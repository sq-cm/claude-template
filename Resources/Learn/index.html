A small studio, not a blank text box

Meet the studio before you meet the software.

This isn't a content generator you point at a blank page. It's a small studio of specialists. Sam the Orchestrator sits at the front desk; 28 named personas work behind them, routing your request to whoever owns that kind of work, checking it before it ships, and remembering what they learned last time. You don't manage the routing. You just describe what you need.

A real walkthrough

How a request flows through the studio

Here's what actually happens when you send a real request, from your message to a finished deliverable. Same team you just met, working in sequence.

You

"I need blog content for a new coffee brand targeting specialty coffee enthusiasts in Australia."

Sam, checking direction first

This is a multi-step ask, so before anyone drafts anything: grill-me asks a few quick questions to pin down audience and scope, then Sam lays out the plan (who's doing what, in what order) for you to approve. You confirm.

Sam, routing

Content strategy first, then copy. Handing this to Sage (Content Strategist) to build the content framework, then Finn (Copywriter) will write from it. Tate (Project Manager), you're on delivery tracking.

Sage, Content Strategist

Before drafting, checking the repo library for content strategy frameworks. Running Checkpoint A with Odin (Senior Adviser) before committing to a structure. Here's the content architecture: three pillar topics, Origin Stories, Brewing Methods, and Ethical Sourcing. Supporting cluster topics mapped to search intent. Brief prepared for Finn.

Finn, Copywriter

Working from Sage's brief. Running Checkpoint B before delivery. First article drafted: "Why Single-Origin Coffee Tastes Different (And Why It Matters)." 800 words, conversational but informed. Ready for Quinn's pass.

Quinn, QA Reviewer

Result: Pass. Article aligns with brief. No unverified claims. Tone consistent. One suggestion: add a source citation on the altitude flavour profile claim. Returning to Sam.

Sam, handoff complete

Article delivered to Projects/[Client]/03 Deliverables/. Quinn flagged one citation, Finn will add it. Let me know when you're ready for article two.

How to address the team

Three ways to talk to the studio. Pick the one that matches how much you already know.

Open request

"I need a first draft of a client welcome email for a new skincare brand."

No @ needed. Sam reads your request and routes it to the best-fit specialist.

Direct address

"@{Copywriter} write a 200-word homepage intro for Meridian Law."

Use @{RoleToken} when you know who you need. Bypasses Sam's routing step.

Both @{Copywriter} and @Finn work, use whichever feels natural.

Multi-step request

"I need a full social calendar for NovaStar Gym, strategy, post copy, and visual direction."

Sam will loop in Sage, Juno, and Cleo in sequence. You'll see the handoffs happen.

Know who, but not how to ask? Once you've worked out who to address, the Prompt Formula Cheat Sheet shows how to phrase the request itself, five slots and a finish line, so it lands first time.

Find yourself

Who this is for

Three of the reasons people end up using this vault. Each card states the trade honestly, catch included.

Marketer, designer, or developer

Someone who does the craft work themselves, copy, design, or code, and wants more of it done without hiring anyone else.

A whole studio, without hiring.

You get specialists in copy, SEO, brand strategy, visual production and analytics on tap. You're still the one running the session, but you're not the only skill set in the room.

Best way to use it: run everyday jobs through the Fast-Path Lane (single-file, reversible work skips the full pipeline). For anything bigger, let Sam route you to @{Copywriter}, @{BrandStrategist}, and so on.

The catch: one session, one Orchestrator. You still bring the work in yourself, you just don't have to be the one doing every part of it.

Business owner or manager

Accountable for what goes out the door, whether that's client work or something a stakeholder inside the business will see.

Nothing reaches a client or stakeholder unchecked.

Every file heading to 03 Deliverables/ goes through the QA Compliance Reviewer first, with an Advisor Checkpoint before drafting and before handoff on checkpoint-eligible work (durable or hard-to-unwind jobs, as opposed to a quick lookup).

Best way to use it: treat the QA Gate (PASS / FLAGGED / BLOCKED) as non-negotiable. Route regulated-category copy through @{LegalComplianceWriter} before it goes near anyone outside the team.

The catch: Advisor Checkpoints and the QA Gate run on the team's most capable model (claude-fable-5): real token cost on every checkpoint-eligible job. Safety isn't free.

Non-technical team member

Needs something done in here without knowing, or wanting to know, how any of it works underneath.

You don't need to know who does what.

Sam figures out routing for you; grill-me asks one question at a time instead of demanding a perfect brief, and plan mode (a preview of the plan you get to approve first) shows the approach before anything happens.

Best way to use it: describe what you want in plain language and let Sam route it. If you're not sure your request is clear, run /prompt-review first, it coaches the ask before you send it.

The catch: this is a Claude Code template, not a polished SaaS product. There's a genuine learning curve to the conventions (folders, checkpoints, what goes where), even with the guardrails softening it.
