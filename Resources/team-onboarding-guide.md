# Welcome to the AI Team — A Guide for New Users

This document is for you if someone handed you access to this system and you want to understand what it is, what it can do for you, and how to start using it without reading a manual.

---

## What this system is

Think of it as a group chat with a team of specialists — except the specialists are AI, they're available any time, and they already know their jobs.

There's a coordinator — the **Orchestrator** — who receives everything you send and routes it to the right person. Behind the Orchestrator is a team of 20 specialists: a copywriter, a content strategist, a UX designer, an SEO specialist, a social media manager, a brand strategist, and more. Each one has a defined area of expertise and clear limits on what they will and won't do. They don't overstep each other's lanes, and they hand work off cleanly between them.

You don't manage the routing. You just talk to the Orchestrator — or directly to whoever you need — and the work gets done.

---

## What the team can do for you

Here's how this maps to work you might already be doing:

**You need a first draft of something** — send a brief to Finn (Copywriter). He'll write to spec and flag anything unclear rather than guess.

**You're working on a client's website content** — Sage (Content Strategist) can audit what exists, identify gaps, and structure a plan. Alex (SEO Specialist) can layer in keyword and search intent guidance. Casey (Webflow Developer) handles the CMS side.

**You're preparing a social media calendar** — Juno (Social Media Manager) can build the calendar. Cleo (Visual AI Producer) can brief out the image and visual direction.

**You need a brand audit or positioning review** — Remi (Brand Strategist) handles messaging architecture, value proposition work, and brand voice documentation.

**You're reviewing a piece of work before it goes to a client** — Quinn (QA Compliance Reviewer) runs a structured review against the brief and flags inconsistencies.

**You want research on a competitor, market, or topic** — the Senior Researcher produces documented research briefs, not bullet-point summaries.

You can also mix these. A real request might go: the Orchestrator routes to Sage for strategy, Sage briefs Finn for copy, the QA Compliance Reviewer reviews before delivery. You're not managing that handoff — the team is.

## How to actually start

1. **Install Claude Code.** Download it from [claude.ai/code](https://claude.ai/code) and sign in with your Anthropic account. If you don't have one, create a free account — your administrator may have already arranged access.

2. **Open the right folder.** In Claude Code, open the workspace folder you've been given access to (it will be named something like `Claude - [Studio Name]`). This is important — the AI team only exists inside this folder.

3. **Say hello to the Orchestrator.** Just type normally. You don't need a special command or syntax. Something like "Hi, I need help writing a client email" is enough. The Orchestrator will respond, tell you who they're handing the work to, and that person will take over.

That's it. You're in.

---

## How to talk to the team

**Open request — the Orchestrator decides who handles it:**
> "I need to write a brief for a new client's brand refresh."

The Orchestrator will assess the request and route it to the best-fit team member.

**Direct address — you choose who handles it:**
> "@Finn I need a 200-word intro paragraph for the Meridian Law homepage. Tone: authoritative but approachable."

The `@Name` format goes directly to that person, bypassing Sam's routing step. Use it when you already know who you need.

**Multi-step request — the Orchestrator coordinates the handoffs:**
> "I need a full social calendar for NovaStar Gym — strategy, post copy, and visual direction for Instagram."

The Orchestrator will loop in the Social Media Manager, Sage, and Cleo in sequence. You'll see the work handed off and built up in stages.

## When someone new is needed

If you ask for something and no one on the current team can cover it, the Orchestrator will tell you there's a gap and ask if you want to bring someone on. If you say yes, the system runs a short hiring process: the Senior Researcher researches the role, the HR Lead builds the persona, and the Orchestrator announces the new hire and adds them to the team. The whole thing usually takes one session.

---

## Things you don't need to worry about

**How it works under the hood.** You don't need to understand language models, prompts, or AI architecture. Talk to the Orchestrator like you'd talk to a coordinator.

**Files and folders.** The team manages its own working files. You'll see outputs appear in the relevant project folders, but you don't need to manage the structure yourself.

**Quality control.** For substantive deliverables, the team runs an internal review process before handing work back to you. You don't need to ask for it — it's built in.

**Getting it perfect first try.** This is a conversation, not a form. If the output isn't quite right, say so and it gets revised. "Make this more formal" or "the client asked for shorter" is a complete instruction.

---

## One thing worth knowing

The team members are specific about their scope. A copywriter won't make strategic decisions; a strategist won't write the final copy. This is intentional — it keeps the quality of each discipline high and makes the work easier to review. If you feel like you're being bounced around, that's the system working correctly. Sam holds the thread.

If you have questions about the team itself — who does what, how to use a feature, or whether someone can help with a specific task — just ask the Orchestrator directly.
