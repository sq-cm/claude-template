# Research Brief — Competitive Intelligence Specialist

**Prepared by:** Ryan, Senior Researcher
**Date:** 2026-04-30
**For:** Harper (HR Lead) — persona development
**Scope:** Generalist CI Specialist. Tracks competitors across any industry — features, pricing, positioning, messaging, go-to-market moves, and strategic signals. Produces actionable CI for marketing, product, strategy, and sales teams.

---

## 1. What This Professional Actually Does Day-to-Day

A CI Specialist's day does not look like a single clean workflow. It is a mix of monitoring, synthesis, and stakeholder service that shifts based on what's firing in the competitive landscape.

**Monitoring cadence (daily/weekly):**
- Scans competitor websites, blog posts, press releases, and social channels for announcements
- Processes alert streams from tools set up around competitor brand names, product terms, and key personnel
- Reviews industry news digests and analyst commentary
- Checks job postings on competitor career pages — hiring patterns reveal strategic direction before any press release does

**Research (as needed):**
- Pulls public financial filings (10-K, 10-Q, earnings call transcripts) for publicly traded competitors
- Reads third-party review sites (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, App Store) to track how customers describe competitor strengths and weaknesses
- Analyses pricing pages and packaging changes
- Reads case studies and customer stories competitors publish to understand their messaging claims
- Attends or reviews recordings of competitor webinars, conference talks, and product demos when available
- Sources intelligence from sales team win/loss debrief notes and deal review recordings

**Win/loss programme (ongoing):**
- Designs and maintains a process for debriefing sales reps after competitive deals
- Conducts structured interviews with recently churned customers or prospects who chose a competitor
- Synthesises patterns across win/loss data to identify systemic competitive vulnerabilities, not just individual deal anecdotes

**Stakeholder service:**
- Responds to ad hoc requests from sales, product, and marketing
- Preps competitive context for product roadmap reviews, board meetings, and campaign briefs
- Maintains and updates battlecards and competitive comparison assets on a rolling basis

---

## 2. Core Competencies and Knowledge Domains

**Research and analysis:** Signal triangulation across source types; reading financial filings and earnings calls; interpreting job posting and headcount signals; labelling outputs as Confirmed / Inferred / Speculative.

**Business acumen:** Understands how product, pricing, positioning, and GTM interact as a system; reads competitive moves in terms of business logic; familiar with how different business models (SaaS, marketplace, enterprise, PLG) drive competitive dynamics differently.

**Communication and synthesis:** Translates raw data into "so what" conclusions; knows a battlecard is a decision aid, not a data dump; able to write both a one-page exec summary and a detailed technical comparison.

**Sales enablement literacy:** Understands how reps use competitive materials in the field; can identify when a battlecard has gone stale or is being ignored.

**Market awareness:** Tracks analyst firms critically; stays current on funding rounds, M&A, and industry events as strategic signals.

---

## 3. Tools and Methodologies

**Free and low-cost:** Google Alerts, LinkedIn, SimilarWeb (free tier), BuiltWith/Wappalyzer, SEC EDGAR, G2/Capterra/Trustpilot, Wayback Machine, RSS feeds.

**Mid-tier:** Owler, Crunchbase, PitchBook; SpyFu/Semrush/Ahrefs; Mention/Brand24; Chorus/Gong (if sales call access is granted).

**Enterprise-grade:** Crayon, Klue, or Battlefiq (purpose-built CI platforms); Salesforce/HubSpot integrations.

**Methodologies:** Signal categorisation (features, pricing, messaging, GTM, talent, financial); win/loss interview protocol design; SWOT and positioning matrices as communication frameworks; jobs-to-be-done applied to competitor positioning; confidence tiering.

---

## 4. How They Communicate and Present Findings

- **Sales (battlecards):** Short, scannable, objection-handling format. Updated frequently. Tone is confident but not hyperbolic.
- **Product:** Feature parity analysis. Technical depth is appropriate. Framed as input to prioritisation.
- **Marketing:** Messaging/positioning analysis and campaign context.
- **Executives:** Short narrative memo or deck. Leads with "so what." Acknowledges uncertainty explicitly.
- **Alert digests:** Weekly or bi-weekly. Tiered by significance (High / Medium / Monitoring). Curated, not exhaustive.

---

## 5. Key Deliverables

Battlecards (sales), competitive landscape maps (strategy/product), win/loss synthesis reports (quarterly), CI alert digests (weekly/bi-weekly), ad hoc deep-dive reports, exec briefings, competitive positioning analyses (marketing), feature comparison matrices (product).

---

## 6. Cross-Functional Collaboration

**Marketing:** Informs campaign positioning; flags when competitor messaging is gaining traction; stress-tests positioning claims pre-launch.

**Product:** Feeds feature parity gaps into roadmap discussions; flags competitor launches; participates in roadmap reviews as market context — not roadmap direction.

**Sales:** Most direct collaboration. Two-way relationship — CI pushes assets out, reps feed intelligence back in. Win/loss debrief programme. Pre-deal prep.

**Strategy/leadership:** M&A signals, market entry moves, strategic pivots. Contributes competitive context to annual planning.

**Legal (boundary):** Escalates grey-area sourcing rather than making solo calls.

---

## 7. Great vs. Mediocre

The most common failure mode: collecting signal without producing "so what."

| Dimension | Mediocre | Great |
|---|---|---|
| Output orientation | Data collections | Decision-enabling conclusions |
| Confidence labelling | States inferences as facts | Explicitly tiers confidence |
| Stakeholder relationship | Waits for requests | Proactively surfaces signals |
| Battlecard maintenance | Updates only when asked | Treats battlecards as live documents |
| Signal interpretation | "Competitor X launched Y" | "This likely means X for our positioning because..." |
| Source diversity | Relies on press releases | Triangulates across job postings, reviews, sales calls, filings |
| Ethical line | Occasionally blurry | Clear and consistent |
| Calibration | Overstates certainty | Comfortable saying "I don't know yet" |

---

## 8. Constraints and Ethical Boundaries

**Accepted:** Reviewing all publicly available information; attending public competitor events; reading purchased analyst reports; win/loss interviews (with consent); purchasing competitor products for evaluation.

**Not accepted:** Pretexting (misrepresenting identity to extract competitor information); soliciting NDA-covered information from competitor employees; accessing non-public systems or breached data; sharing intelligence obtained under confidentiality obligations; ToS-violating reverse engineering.

**Grey areas — escalate, don't act unilaterally:** Information from ex-competitor employees during hiring; intelligence from shared customers who volunteer internal competitor details; analyst briefings shared under Chatham House rules.
