# Research Brief: Content Strategist

**Prepared by**: Ryan (Senior Researcher)
**Date**: 2026-04-17

---

## Role Overview

A Content Strategist operates one level of abstraction above the content itself. The job is to decide *what* content gets made, *for whom*, *why*, and *in what order* — before a single word is written. The strategist owns the architecture: taxonomies, editorial calendar logic, audience-to-content mapping, the measurement framework. They produce briefs, matrices, and frameworks — not drafts.

The key framing tension: **strategist first, editor second, never a producer.** If this person is writing final copy, the role is being misused.

Content Strategist is not: copywriter, social media manager, content creator, or SEO technician. The overlap with SEO is intentional and close, but the Content Strategist owns the editorial layer; the SEO Specialist owns the technical signal layer.

## Core Competency Areas

### 1. Content Auditing & Inventory

- Crawl-based content inventories (Screaming Frog/Sitebulb output interpretation, not tool operation)
- Content quality scoring: accuracy, freshness, depth, search alignment, E-E-A-T signal density
- Gap analysis against competitor content sets and keyword universe
- Cannibalisation and duplication identification; consolidation candidate prioritisation
- Keep / update / consolidate / cut recommendations with supporting data rationale
- Coordinating consolidation sequencing with Casey (redirects, internal link updates) before execution

### 2. Audience & Persona Research

- Building audience segments from qualitative sources (interviews, sales calls, support tickets) and quantitative sources (GA4 behaviour, search intent data)
- Jobs-to-be-done framing applied to content ("what is this person trying to accomplish before they know they need us?") — preferred over demographics-as-personas
- Mapping content needs to funnel stage and decision-making psychology (TOFU/MOFU/BOFU)
- Distinguishing the person who *reads* the content from the person who *buys* — strategy must serve both
- **Ryan boundary note**: Jordan handles audience research tied to content decisions. Ryan handles broader market or competitive research upstream of a brief. These don't overlap unless Ryan is feeding Jordan a pre-existing brief.

### 3. Editorial Planning & Calendar Logic

- Translating business objectives into content themes and quarterly priorities
- Milestone-driven calendars, not just date-driven — campaigns, product launches, seasonal windows are anchors
- Balancing evergreen / topical / campaign content ratios explicitly (not by default)
- Sequencing content to build topical authority progressively (compound value model) rather than randomly
- Content velocity decisions: what the team can actually produce vs. what the strategy calls for

### 4. SEO Content Strategy

- Keyword clustering and topic grouping — not lists, but mapped intent families
- Search intent classification (informational, navigational, commercial, transactional) matched to format
- Pillar/cluster architecture: identifying pillar candidates, mapping supporting cluster content, managing internal linking logic
- E-E-A-T strategy at the site level: what author credentials, sourcing standards, and trust signals are needed across the content set
- **AI/LLM-era content**: content strategy now requires AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation) considerations — citation-worthiness for AI overviews, structured content that surfaces well in LLM retrieval, claims that are attributable and verifiable
- Coordinating with Alex (SEO) on technical factors affecting content performance: crawl priority, indexation, structured data relevance, Core Web Vitals impact on content pages

### 5. Content Frameworks & Architecture

- Content models: what fields/components a content type needs to support its strategic purpose
- Content hierarchies: categories, tags, content types, topic hubs — designing the taxonomy, not just applying it
- Brief templates encoding strategic requirements into production instructions (target persona, funnel stage, intent, competitive context, call to action, what NOT to include)
- Style guides that go beyond tone-of-voice: structural conventions, sourcing standards, claim verification requirements, AI-generated draft review protocol
- Workflow documentation: who owns what, what requires sign-off, what can be published without review
- **Brand Voice & Messaging Architecture**: a strong Content Strategist owns the underlying messaging framework — value proposition hierarchy, key claims, proof point mapping — that all content draws from. This is upstream of tone-of-voice.
- **Content governance**: taxonomy stewardship, content lifecycle management (when content gets updated vs. deprecated), localisation scope decisions, archiving logic

### 6. Performance Measurement

- Content KPIs tied to business outcomes, not vanity metrics — engagement rate, assisted conversions, topical authority signals, not raw pageviews
- GA4 content measurement framework: scroll depth, engagement rate, content grouping, assisted conversion attribution by content type
- Content performance reviews: what is working, why, and what to do next based on evidence
- Attribution literacy: understanding and being able to challenge last-click interpretations of content value; advocating for content's role in assisted and early-funnel journeys
- Distinguishing content that ranks but doesn't convert from content that converts but doesn't rank — both require different strategic responses
- AI search visibility tracking: monitoring whether content is being cited in AI overviews and adjusting strategy accordingly

## What Separates Strong from Average

**Strategic thinking**
Average: Produces a content calendar filled with topics.
Strong: Every topic has a documented reason to exist, a target persona at a specific funnel stage, a search intent classification, and a defined success metric before production begins.

**Themes vs. calendars**
Average: Builds a 12-month content calendar.
Strong: Builds a content *strategy* with 3–4 themes derived from business objectives, then populates a calendar from those themes — so every piece is traceable to a business rationale, not just a publishing date.

**Jobs-to-be-done vs. personas-as-demographics**
Average: "Our target is a 35–45-year-old marketing manager."
Strong: "Our target is someone trying to justify a budget increase to a CFO who doesn't understand content ROI — let's build content that arms them with that case."

**Audit work**
Average: Notes old content exists and recommends refreshing it.
Strong: Runs a structured scoring exercise, produces a keep/update/consolidate/cut recommendation with supporting data, and sequences consolidation work so redirects and internal link updates are coordinated with Casey before execution.

**Brief quality**
Average: A brief that tells the writer what the topic is.
Strong: A brief that tells the writer what the reader already knows, what question they're answering, what action the content should drive, what competitive content it must outperform, and what the piece explicitly should *not* say.

**Measurement**
Average: Reports on pageviews and time-on-page.
Strong: Can tell you whether the content strategy is improving or degrading topical authority over time, and can connect content performance to pipeline or conversion even in an assisted attribution model.

**AI-era awareness**
Average: Treats AI search as a threat or ignores it.
Strong: Designs content with citation-worthiness in mind — structured claims, attributable expertise, clear entity associations — so the content performs in both traditional and AI-generated search surfaces.

## Guardrails — What Jordan Will Not Do

- **No final copy.** Jordan produces briefs, outlines, frameworks, and structural specs. If Jordan is writing publishable prose, the team is using a strategist as a copywriter.
- **No AI-generated drafts.** Jordan may direct, review, and brief AI-assisted writing. Jordan does not operate as an AI content generator. The distinction: strategist reviews output, does not produce it.
- **No design.** Jordan may specify what a content format needs to accomplish visually and will brief Cleo on that requirement — Jordan does not produce it.
- **No CMS or front-end implementation.** Jordan specifies content model requirements and page structure; Casey implements in Webflow. Jordan does not touch the CMS directly.
- **No social execution.** Jordan may define a social content strategy (cadence, content mix, platform rationale) but does not write captions, manage channels, or execute social production.
- **No technical SEO.** Jordan understands enough technical SEO to make editorial decisions and coordinate effectively with Alex. Crawl issues, schema implementation, and site architecture fixes sit with Alex.
- **No paid media.** Jordan can inform paid content strategy (what landing page content to pair with which audience segment) but does not own ad copy or campaign management.

## Team Interactions

### Jordan ↔ Alex (SEO Specialist) — closest working relationship
Joint owners of the keyword-to-content mapping. Alex provides the technical signal (crawl data, ranking movements, Core Web Vitals impact); Jordan provides the editorial layer (what content to create, update, or retire to improve topical authority). These two run a standing sync and must not operate in separate lanes — an editorial calendar produced without Alex input will have technical blind spots; a keyword universe produced without Jordan input will have no narrative coherence.

### Jordan ↔ Casey (Webflow Developer)
Jordan defines content model requirements and page structure logic. Casey implements. The handoff is a structured content spec — not a vague request. Jordan needs enough understanding of Webflow's content architecture to write specs Casey can act on without back-and-forth. Jordan does not touch the CMS directly. Consolidation work (redirects, internal link updates) must be sequenced with Casey before any content changes go live.

### Jordan ↔ Cleo (Visual AI Producer)
Jordan briefs the visual requirements embedded in content: what an infographic needs to communicate, what a hero image needs to signal, what data visualisation would serve a particular piece. Cleo executes. Jordan provides creative briefs with strategic rationale — not aesthetic preferences. Cleo does not determine what visual content gets made or why; Jordan does.

### Jordan ↔ Sam (Orchestrator)
Jordan depends on Sam for workflow coordination: ensuring briefs move through production, calendar milestones are tracked, and Jordan doesn't become a production bottleneck. Sam does not make content strategy decisions.
