High Severity Findings

High

H1 — Broken redirect chain on three campaign landing pages

What we found

The URLs /services/growth, /services/retention, and /services/strategy were migrated in April but the old redirect rules now chain through three hops before resolving — or return 404 on mobile user agents.

Why it matters

Google drops pages from its index after sustained 4xx responses. Paid traffic sent to these URLs will land on an error page, wasting budget and damaging Quality Score.

Recommendation

Flatten to a single 301 redirect directly to the current canonical URL. Test with both desktop and mobile user agents before campaign activation.

Technical detail — redirect chain trace 
/services/growth  →  301  /services/growth/  →  301  /growth-services/  →  404 (mobile UA)
/services/retention  →  301  /services/retention/  →  301  /retention-services/  →  404 (mobile UA)
/services/strategy  →  301  /old-strategy  →  301  /services/strategy-consulting/  →  200

Confirmed with curl -L -I -A "Mozilla/5.0 (iPhone; CPU iPhone OS 17_0)". The Webflow publish pipeline is appending a trailing slash that conflicts with the existing Cloudflare redirect rules.

High

H2 — Largest Contentful Paint exceeds 4s on mobile for all service pages

What we found

LCP measures 4.2–5.8s on Moto G4 (4G throttled) for all six /services/* pages. The LCP element is a full-bleed hero image served as PNG at 2400px wide with no responsive srcset.

Why it matters

Google's Core Web Vitals threshold is 2.5s. Pages failing LCP are demoted in mobile search rankings. This is the primary drag on the site's 62/100 CWV score.

Recommendation

Convert hero images to WebP, implement srcset with 400/800/1200px breakpoints, add fetchpriority="high" to the LCP image element, and preload the above-fold image in <head>.

Technical detail — image asset analysis

Hero image at /assets/hero-services.png: 2400 × 1350px, 1.4 MB. No WebP variant. No srcset. loading="lazy" incorrectly applied to the LCP element (should be eager). Estimated savings with WebP + correct sizing: ~1.1 MB, projected LCP improvement to 2.1–2.6s based on comparable migrations.

High

H3 — Duplicate title tags across 11 blog posts

What we found

Eleven blog posts share identical <title> values — all reading "Insights | Sample Client". The CMS template is missing the post-title variable in its head template.

Why it matters

Duplicate titles prevent Google from distinguishing post intent. Click-through rate from search is suppressed because the SERP title defaults to the URL slug rather than the post headline.

Recommendation

Update the CMS blog template to populate <title> from the post title field using the pattern: [Post Title] | Insights | Sample Client. Max 60 characters recommended.

High

H4 — robots.txt blocking /services/* from Googlebot

What we found

A Disallow: /services/ rule in robots.txt is preventing all service pages from being crawled. This appears to be a legacy rule carried over from the staging environment.

Why it matters

Service pages are the commercial core of the site. If they cannot be crawled, they cannot rank. Google Search Console confirms zero impressions for /services/* in the past 28 days.

Recommendation

Remove the Disallow: /services/ rule. Verify in GSC that pages are submitted for re-indexing. Establish a robots.txt review as part of the deployment checklist going forward.

High

H5 — Missing structured data on all service pages

What we found

No Service, Organization, or BreadcrumbList schema markup exists anywhere on the site. Competitors rank with rich results (service cards, sitelinks) in the same keyword set.

Why it matters

Structured data is a primary driver of rich results eligibility. Without it, the site cannot compete for the enhanced SERP features its competitors currently occupy.

Recommendation

Implement Service schema on each service page and Organization schema on the homepage. Add BreadcrumbList to all interior pages. Use JSON-LD embedded in the <head>.

High

H6 — No canonical tags on paginated blog archive

What we found

The blog archive at /insights/ paginates to /insights/?page=2 etc. without canonical tags. All paginated variants are treated as separate pages by Googlebot, diluting PageRank.

Why it matters

Pagination without canonicalisation causes index bloat and splits link equity across near-duplicate pages.

Recommendation

Add <link rel="canonical"> to each paginated page pointing to itself (self-referencing canonical). Add rel="next" / rel="prev" for pagination signals.

Medium Severity Findings

Medium

M1 — Alt text missing on 23 images across the blog

What we found

23 inline images in blog posts have empty or absent alt attributes. Most are illustrative diagrams and charts, not decorative.

Why it matters

Missing alt text is both an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.1.1) and an SEO gap — Google Images cannot index image content without descriptive alt text.

Recommendation

Audit all blog images and add descriptive alt text. For purely decorative images, use alt="" explicitly. Update the content brief template to require alt text as a mandatory field.

Medium

M2 — Internal linking: 14 service sub-pages have zero inbound internal links

What we found

14 pages under /services/*/ are reachable only via the sitemap. No page on the site links to them organically.

Why it matters

Orphaned pages receive no PageRank from the internal link graph, suppressing their ranking potential regardless of content quality.

Recommendation

Identify 2–3 relevant parent pages or blog posts that should contextually reference each orphaned page. Add in-text links. Update the main services navigation to include tier-2 pages.

M3 — Meta descriptions missing or auto-generated on 19 pages

What we found

19 pages have meta descriptions that are either absent or generated by pulling the first sentence of body copy — resulting in descriptions that are truncated, off-topic, or duplicate.

Why it matters

Google uses meta descriptions as the default SERP snippet. Poor descriptions reduce CTR, which is a soft ranking signal.

Recommendation

Write unique meta descriptions for all commercial pages (services, homepage, about, contact) at 130–155 characters with a clear value proposition and implicit CTA.

Medium

M4 — Heading hierarchy broken on 8 pages (multiple H1s, skipped levels)

What we found

Eight pages contain 2–3 H1 elements each. Several pages jump from H2 directly to H4, skipping H3 entirely. This appears to be a visual styling override applied at the CMS level.

Why it matters

Broken heading hierarchy is an accessibility issue (WCAG 1.3.1) and weakens semantic signals for Googlebot. Screen reader users rely on heading structure for page navigation.

Recommendation

Audit heading structure across all pages. Enforce single H1 per page. Apply visual size variations via CSS class rather than semantic heading level. Update the CMS rich-text component's allowed heading levels.

Resolved Since Last Review

R1 — Sitemap XML errors resolved. The sitemap previously contained 12 URLs returning 301 redirects. These have been updated to canonical final destinations. All 48 submitted URLs now return 200.

R2 — HTTPS mixed content warnings cleared. All 6 mixed-content warnings (HTTP asset references on HTTPS pages) identified in the previous audit have been resolved. The site now loads with a clean HTTPS certificate across all pages.

R3 — Contact form accessible via keyboard. The contact page form previously trapped keyboard focus. This has been corrected — all form fields and the submit button are now reachable and operable via keyboard, and focus order matches visual order.
