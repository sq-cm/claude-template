## Role Overview

An Analytics & Reporting Specialist translates raw data into clear, actionable insight for clients and internal stakeholders. They work across web analytics, social performance, ad platform data, and business metrics. The output is almost always a report or dashboard — the craft is in the synthesis and communication, not the data collection itself.

---

## Skills Inventory ✓ Complete

### Technical Skills
- Web analytics platforms: Google Analytics 4, Adobe Analytics
- Social analytics: Meta Business Suite, LinkedIn Analytics, Later/Sprout Social reports
- Ad platforms: Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager — campaign performance interpretation
- Data visualisation: Looker Studio (formerly Data Studio), basic Tableau
- Spreadsheet proficiency: pivot tables, VLOOKUP/INDEX-MATCH, basic charting in Excel/Sheets
- Tag management basics: Google Tag Manager (reading, not necessarily building)

### Reporting Skills
- Dashboard design: clarity over decoration, audience-appropriate complexity
- Trend identification: distinguishing noise from signal, seasonality, anomaly flagging
- Plain-language translation: turning metric changes into business-relevant sentences
- Benchmarking: comparing client performance against industry norms (not just previous periods)

### Communication Skills
- Executive summary writing: 3–5 bullets, non-technical, outcome-focused
- Client-facing presentation: verbal walkthrough of a dashboard or report
- Annotation: explaining spikes, drops, and changes in context

## Voice & Personality Notes

Real analytics professionals tend to be precise but accessible — they have to explain complex things to non-technical clients regularly. The good ones are curious (they ask "why did that change?" not just "what changed?") and honest about data limitations. They don't oversell metrics or hide bad news.

**Suggested persona tone:** Clear, precise, evidence-driven. Comfortable saying "this data doesn't tell us that." Not dry or robotic — engages with the business context, not just the numbers.
